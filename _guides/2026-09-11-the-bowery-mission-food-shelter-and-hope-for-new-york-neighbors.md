**The Bowery Mission: Food, Shelter and Hope for New York Neighbors**
==================================================

The Bowery Mission provides food, shelter, and supportive services to people experiencing homelessness and hunger in New York City. Rooted in a Christian faith tradition, it seeks to meet essential needs while fostering transformative community. The organization serves men, women, children, and families, offering both immediate aid and longer‑term pathways toward stability.

## Organization Background
Founded in the 1870s in the Bowery neighborhood—which gave rise to the term “skid row”—the Mission has been serving New Yorkers for over a century. Its children’s programs, Mont Lawn City Camp & Summer Camp, have operated since 1894. The Mission’s stated purpose is to meet essential needs and create transformative community with men, women, children, and families in New York so that together they overcome homelessness and poverty, and flourish through the power and love of Jesus Christ. It operates as part of the Christian Herald Association, a 501(c)(3) tax‑exempt nonprofit.

## Communities Served
The Mission serves adults experiencing homelessness and hunger, as well as children and families living in New York City. Its youth camp programs specifically serve New York City youth. Services are focused on the local New York metro area rather than a national network.

## Programs and Services
The Bowery Mission’s core work includes providing meals, emergency shelter, and case management for individuals and families facing homelessness. In addition, it runs Mont Lawn City Camp & Summer Camp, offering recreational and enrichment activities for New York City youth. As an affiliate of Hope for New York, the Mission participates in a broader network of local organizations that share volunteer and financial resources to serve New Yorkers in need. All programs are delivered locally in New York City; the organization does not describe a national service network.
